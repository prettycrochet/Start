Halloween Crochet 2026: Vintage & Dark Botanical Instead of Neon Spooky – Plus a Free Vintage Ghost Pattern

By PrettyCrochet · Published on October 2, 2026 · Reading time: approx. 7 minutes

In short

Halloween crochet in 2026 is quieter, warmer and prettier. Instead of bright orange and black, muted vintage colors and dark botanical motifs are in demand – moths, mushrooms, ferns, ravens and moon phases. Add to that unusual amigurumi, mosaic pillows and C2C blankets and crocheted costumes. Further down you'll find a free beginner pattern for a vintage ghost with a ruffled hem.

October is here – and with it the question that comes up every year: how much Halloween is too much? I love spooky decor, just not the plastic skeleton from the discount store. So I'm delighted that the crochet world is heading in a direction that suits me perfectly this year: away from neon and kitsch, towards decorations that look as if they've been sitting on Grandma's windowsill for decades. Here are the trends – and a little ghost to make yourself.

1. Vintage Halloween: what does the look involve?

You don't need genuine old patterns for vintage Halloween. The look comes from faded colors, fine openwork, clear silhouettes (ghosts, bats, witches, jack-o'-lantern faces) and restrained embellishment. Typical projects are doilies, coasters, garlands, small shelf figures and spiderwebs.

My tip: repeat two or three colors across all your projects. That way your decorations look like a collection that has grown over the years.

Which colors suit vintage Halloween?

  • Cream & ivory
  • Soot black
  • Burnt orange
  • Rust
  • Mustard yellow
  • Faded chartreuse
  • Dark plum
  • Moss green

The color swatches are a guide – compare them with real yarn in the shop.

2. Dark botanical: what is it?

Dark botanical is the style I'm most excited about this year. Instead of pumpkins, black cats and witch hats, it's all about moths, mushrooms, ferns, poppy pods, blackberry branches, ravens, seeds and moon phases – imagery straight out of an old natural history book. The big advantage: these decorations don't only work on October 31; they still look lovely well into winter.

Project ideas: a moon phase garland, crocheted fern leaves for a wreath, moths as wall decor or mini mushrooms. For the mushrooms you can use my mini mushroom pattern – in rust, moss green and cream it fits the dark botanical look perfectly.

3. Unusual amigurumi instead of standard characters

Figures with their own personality are what people want: a ghost with a ruffled hem, a raven with a crescent moon, a moth with openwork wings. If you already have some experience, try reinterpreting familiar Halloween characters with unexpected colors or details.

4. Mosaic pillows and C2C blankets with Halloween motifs

For anyone who loves graphic crochet: mosaic crochet (two-color patterns made with dropped stitches) and C2C graphgans (blankets worked diagonally from a grid chart) are popular this year for Halloween pillows and sofa blankets. These are more intermediate projects – and probably for next year if you're only starting now.

5. Crocheted costumes and Halloween cardigans

The community is full of handmade costumes this fall – from a baby's first Halloween outfit to witch hats – and cardigans with Halloween motifs, for example made from joined hexagons. If you prefer something wearable, crochet a cardigan in vintage colors that you'll still reach for after Halloween.

Pattern: Vintage ghost with a ruffled hem

Size
approx. 2.5 in (6–7 cm) tall
Time
about 1–1.5 hours
Level
Beginner
Yarn
Cotton in cream or ivory (approx. 15–20 g), a little black for the face
Tools
2.5–3 mm hook, toy stuffing, stitch marker, tapestry needle

Abbreviations (US terms)

MR = magic ring · sc = single crochet · inc = 2 sc in the same stitch · dec = single crochet 2 together · BLO = back loop only · dc = double crochet · sl st = slip stitch. All abbreviations are listed in the abbreviations guide.

Work in continuous rounds from the top down and mark the first stitch of each round. Want to brush up on the basics? Try the crochet basics or the learning path.

Head and body (cream)

  1. MR, 6 sc (6)
  2. Inc × 6 (12)
  3. (Sc, inc) × 6 (18)
  4. (2 sc, inc) × 6 (24)
  5. Rounds 5–12: sc around (24) – that's 8 rounds

Face

With black yarn, embroider two small eyes between rounds 7 and 8, about 4 stitches apart, and a small oval mouth in round 9. Embroidered eyes look more vintage and are safer than safety eyes if there are small children in the house.

Base

  1. BLO: (2 sc, dec) × 6 (18) – the front loops of round 12 stay free for the hem
  2. (Sc, dec) × 6 (12) – stuff firmly now
  3. Dec × 6 (6)

Cut the yarn, weave it through the last 6 stitches, pull tight and weave in the end.

Ruffled hem

  1. Turn the ghost upside down. Join new yarn with a sl st in any free front loop of round 12.
  2. (Skip 1 st, 5 dc in the next st, skip 1 st, sl st in the next st) × 6. The last sl st lands in the joining stitch.
  3. Weave in the ends. You now have 6 little shells – just like the hem of a bedsheet.

Garland variation: add a small chain loop to the top of the head and thread several ghosts onto a cord, alternating with mini mushrooms.

Frequently asked questions about Halloween crochet

01

What are the Halloween crochet trends for 2026?

In 2026 the trend is moving away from bright orange and black towards vintage Halloween with muted colors and openwork, dark botanical motifs such as moths, mushrooms, ferns, ravens and moon phases, unusual amigurumi instead of standard characters, mosaic pillows and C2C blankets with Halloween motifs, and crocheted costumes and Halloween cardigans.

02

Which colors work for vintage Halloween decor?

Warm, muted tones are typical: cream and ivory, soot black, burnt orange and rust, mustard yellow, faded chartreuse, dark plum, moss green, and charcoal or brown-black. Repeating two or three of these colors across several projects gives a cohesive, collected look.

03

What is dark botanical?

Dark botanical is a decor style with dark, natural-history imagery: moths, mushrooms, ferns, poppy pods, blackberry branches, ravens, seeds and moon phases. The advantage for crochet projects is that the decor doesn't only work at Halloween but still looks good well into winter.

04

What can beginners crochet for Halloween?

Small amigurumi such as a ghost, flat motifs for garlands or coasters, and mini mushrooms are all good choices. A simple amigurumi ghost only needs the magic ring, single crochet, increases and decreases and takes about an hour and a half.

05

When should I start crocheting Halloween decorations?

For small decorations like ghosts, mushrooms or garlands, early October is plenty of time. Many people start bigger projects such as blankets, cardigans or costumes in late summer.
