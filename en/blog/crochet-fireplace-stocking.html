Crochet a Big Christmas Stocking for the Mantel – Free Pattern

By PrettyCrochet · Published November 8, 2026 · Reading time: about 6 minutes

In short

The easiest way to crochet a big Christmas stocking is from two identical panels: crochet both in rows of half double crochet from the top down, sew them together all around and add a cuff in rounds with a hanging loop. With worsted/aran weight yarn and a 5 mm (H-8) hook it comes out about 20 in (50 cm) tall (approximate). Plan on about 7–9 hours – starting in early November, two or three evenings a week get it done by December 5.

By the evening of December 5, the stocking can already be up – ideally big and on the mantel. For this big fireplace stocking you only need rows of half double crochet: two identical panels, one seam all around and a ribbed cuff. Here you'll find the free pattern with stitch counts for every row, a diagram and a schedule to finish by St. Nicholas Day.

Boot or stocking: where does the tradition come from?

St. Nicholas Day on December 6 honors Saint Nicholas, who was bishop of Myra in the 4th century. In Germany, children put polished shoes outside the door on the night before December 6, and St. Nicholas fills them.

The stocking comes from a legend of unknown origin: a widowed father couldn't afford dowries for his three daughters. Saint Nicholas secretly helped by filling their freshly washed stockings, drying by the fire, with gold coins. In a poem from 1823, stockings are already hung by the chimney for St. Nick (sources below). A big stocking combines both: hang it on December 5 instead of a boot, then keep it up until Christmas.

Pattern: big fireplace stocking with stitch counts

Size
about 20 in (50 cm) tall, about 9 in (23 cm) wide, foot about 13.5 in (34 cm) (approximate)
Time
about 7–9 hours including sewing (approximate)
Level
Advanced beginner (rows of half double crochet, decreases, one round for the cuff)
Yarn
worsted/aran weight yarn, e.g. a wool blend or cotton; an estimated 350 g (12.5 oz) in the main color and about 80 g (3 oz) in cream – buy an extra ball to be safe
Gauge
14 hdc × 10 rows = 4 × 4 in (10 × 10 cm)
Tools
5 mm (H-8) hook, large-eye tapestry needle, pins or stitch markers to hold the panels, scissors

Abbreviations (US terms)

ch = chain · hdc = half double crochet · sc = single crochet · sl st = slip stitch · dec = hdc2tog (half double crochet 2 together) · BLO = back loop only. All abbreviations and chart symbols are in the abbreviations guide, and rows and turning chains are explained in the crochet basics. The stitch counter makes counting rows easy.

1. Side panels (make 2 the same, main color)

  1. Row 1: ch 34, starting in the 3rd ch from the hook, 32 hdc (32)
  2. Rows 2–26: ch 2 (does not count as a stitch), turn, 32 hdc (32)
  3. Row 27: ch 18, turn, starting in the 3rd ch from the hook, 16 hdc, then 32 hdc across the row (48)
  4. Rows 28–37: ch 2, turn, 48 hdc (48)
  5. Row 38: ch 2, turn, dec, 44 hdc, dec (46)
  6. Row 39: ch 2, turn, dec, 42 hdc, dec (44)
  7. Row 40: ch 2, turn, dec, 40 hdc, dec (42)

Fasten off, leaving a long tail, and make the second panel the same way. The foundation edge is the top, the side with the chain extension becomes the toe, and the opposite lower corner is the heel.

2. Sew the sides together

  1. Place both panels on top of each other so the edges line up and both toes point the same way
  2. With a tapestry needle and main-color yarn, whipstitch all the way around: from the top corner on the heel side over the heel, sole and toe to the top corner of the front edge
  3. Leave the top edge open and turn the stocking inside out so the seam is on the inside

Half double crochet looks similar on both sides – that's why you can make both panels the same. Go into every stitch around the curves so the seam doesn't pull.

3. Cuff (cream, in rounds)

  1. Round 1: join new yarn at the side seam above the heel, 1 sc in each foundation stitch of both panels, join with a sl st (64)
  2. Rounds 2–10: ch 2 (does not count), 64 hdc in the back loop only, join with a sl st in the 1st hdc (64)

Weave in the end. Working in the back loop creates the horizontal ridges.

4. Hanging loop

  1. Join yarn at the top of the cuff above the heel seam, ch 20 and close with a sl st in the same stitch (loop)
  2. Work about 26 sc around the loop and finish with a sl st (about 26)

Weave in securely – on the mantel, the loop carries the full weight of the filled stocking.

Tip: Pin the two panels together with a few stitch markers before sewing so nothing shifts. If you'd like a name, embroider it onto the front panel with surface slip stitches before you sew the panels together.

Can I make the stocking bigger or smaller?

  • Longer or shorter: work more or fewer rows in rows 2–26. Each row adds about 0.4 in (1 cm) (approximate).
  • Wider or narrower: chain 1 more or fewer for each stitch you add or remove. The cuff then has twice as many stitches as one panel.
  • Different yarn: thicker yarn (chunky, 6–7 mm hook) makes a bigger stocking from the same pattern, thinner yarn a smaller one.

Can I finish the fireplace stocking by December 6?

Yes, if you start now. At about 7–9 hours (approximate), two or three evenings a week are enough:

WeekWhat
1Gauge swatch, first panel
2Second panel
3Embroider a name, sew together, cuff
4Hanging loop, weave in ends, buffer time – and hang it on December 5
