Crochet a Christmas Stocking in One Piece: A Big Seamless Fireplace Stocking

By PrettyCrochet · Published November 8, 2026 · Reading time: about 7 minutes

In short

You can crochet a Christmas stocking in one piece by working in the round: start at the toe, crochet the foot and leg as a tube and leave a chain gap where the heel goes at the end. That way there are no side seams. For a big fireplace stocking (about 20 in / 50 cm tall, approximate), half double crochet in continuous rounds, worsted/aran weight yarn and a 5 mm (H-8) hook are all you need – plan on about 8–10 hours.

By the evening of December 5, the stocking can already be up – ideally big and on the mantel. This time I wanted a stocking without seams, so I looked into how to crochet a Christmas stocking in one piece and compared four heel methods. The result is a pattern for a big fireplace stocking in continuous rounds, with the heel added at the very end.

How do you crochet a stocking in one piece?

All of these methods work in the round, as a tube with no side seam. The difference is the heel – it gives the stocking its bend. In freely available patterns you'll mostly find these four approaches (examples in the sources):

MethodHow it worksGood for
Afterthought heelIn one round you chain across half the stitches and skip them. At the end you work rounds with decreases into the opening.beginners who can work in the round; toe and heel work the same way
Heel worked in rowsPartway through, the heel is worked back and forth over some of the stitches, then you continue in rounds.a clearly shaped heel; a bit more counting
Shaped heel with increases and decreasesStitches are increased and then decreased on one side over a few rounds – without cutting the yarn.crocheting straight through in one go; a flatter heel
Sole firstAn oval sole is worked around a foundation chain first, then the boot is crocheted upward.small St. Nicholas boots with a flat bottom

Why an afterthought heel for a big fireplace stocking?

  • No seams: the whole stocking is one tube – nothing to sew or crochet together.
  • One technique, two places: the toe is shaped with increases, the heel with decreases in the same pattern.
  • Easy to adjust: change the length with the number of leg rounds, without recalculating the heel.
  • Great for big stockings: the stitch count stays the same from foot to cuff – you just keep going round after round.

Pattern: big one-piece fireplace stocking

Size
about 20 in (50 cm) tall, about 9 in (23 cm) wide, foot about 13.5 in (34 cm) (approximate)
Time
about 8–10 hours (approximate)
Level
Advanced beginner who can work in the round (magic ring, half double crochet, increases and decreases)
Yarn
worsted/aran weight yarn, e.g. a wool blend or cotton; an estimated 450 g (16 oz) in the main color and about 80 g (3 oz) in cream – buy an extra ball to be safe
Gauge
14 hdc × 10 rounds = 4 × 4 in (10 × 10 cm)
Tools
5 mm (H-8) hook, 2 stitch markers, tapestry needle, scissors

Abbreviations (US terms)

MR = magic ring · hdc = half double crochet · sc = single crochet · ch = chain · sl st = slip stitch · inc = 2 hdc in the same stitch · dec = hdc2tog (half double crochet 2 together) · BLO = back loop only. All abbreviations and chart symbols are in the abbreviations guide, and the magic ring and continuous rounds are explained in the crochet basics. With 70 rounds, the stitch counter helps you keep track.

1. Toe (main color)

  1. Round 1: 8 hdc in the MR (8)
  2. Round 2: inc 8 times (16)
  3. Round 3: (1 hdc, inc) 8 times (24)
  4. Round 4: (2 hdc, inc) 8 times (32)
  5. Round 5: (3 hdc, inc) 8 times (40)
  6. Round 6: 40 hdc (40)
  7. Round 7: (4 hdc, inc) 8 times (48)
  8. Round 8: 48 hdc (48)
  9. Round 9: (5 hdc, inc) 8 times (56)
  10. Round 10: 56 hdc (56)
  11. Round 11: (6 hdc, inc) 8 times (64)
  12. Round 12: 64 hdc (64)

Work in continuous rounds without joining – the stitch marker shows you where each round begins.

2. Foot and heel opening

  1. Rounds 13–26: 64 hdc (64)
  2. Round 27: 32 hdc, then ch 32 loosely and skip the next 32 stitches (32 hdc + 32 ch)
  3. Round 28: 32 hdc, then 1 hdc in each ch (64)

The skipped stitches and the chain form the opening where you'll add the heel at the end. Keep the chains really loose, or the stocking will pucker at this spot.

3. Leg

  1. Rounds 29–60: 64 hdc (64)

That's 32 rounds. For a shorter or longer stocking, simply work fewer or more rounds here.

4. Cuff (cream)

  1. Round 61: change to cream, 64 sc (64)
  2. Rounds 62–70: 64 hdc in the back loop only (64)

Finish with 2 sl st in the next stitches to smooth out the step of the continuous rounds, then weave in the end.

5. Heel (main color, worked last)

  1. Round 1: join yarn at one corner of the opening, 32 hdc in the skipped stitches and 32 hdc in the underside of the chain (64)
  2. Round 2: (6 hdc, dec) 8 times (56)
  3. Round 3: 56 hdc (56)
  4. Round 4: (5 hdc, dec) 8 times (48)
  5. Round 5: 48 hdc (48)
  6. Round 6: (4 hdc, dec) 8 times (40)
  7. Round 7: 40 hdc (40)
  8. Round 8: (3 hdc, dec) 8 times (32)
  9. Round 9: (2 hdc, dec) 8 times (24)
  10. Round 10: (1 hdc, dec) 8 times (16)
  11. Round 11: dec 8 times (8)

Cut the yarn, weave it through the last 8 stitches, pull tight and weave in the end. Close any small gaps at the two corners of the opening with the starting tail.

6. Hanging loop

  1. Join yarn at the top of the cuff above the heel, ch 20 and close with a sl st in the same stitch (loop)
  2. Work about 26 sc around the loop and finish with a sl st (about 26)

Weave in securely – on the mantel, the loop carries the full weight of the filled stocking.

Tip: In round 27, put a second stitch marker in the first skipped stitch. That way you'll find the heel opening right away later.

Can I make the stocking bigger or smaller?

  • Longer or shorter: work more or fewer rounds in the leg (rounds 29–60). Each round adds about 0.4 in (1 cm) (approximate).
  • Longer foot: work more rounds in rounds 13–26 before the heel opening.
  • Wider or narrower: change the stitch count in steps of 8, for example 56 instead of 64: stop the toe after round 10, chain over 28 stitches in round 27, work 56 stitches in the first heel round and then continue with heel round 4.
  • Different yarn: thicker yarn (chunky, 6–7 mm hook) makes a bigger stocking from the same pattern, thinner yarn a smaller one.

Can I finish the fireplace stocking by December 6?

Yes, if you start now. At about 8–10 hours (approximate), two or three evenings a week are enough:

WeekWhat
1Gauge swatch, toe, foot and heel opening
2Leg up to about round 45
3Finish the leg, cuff
4Heel, hanging loop, embroider a name – and hang it on December 5

Frequently asked questions about one-piece crochet stockings

01

Can you crochet a Christmas stocking in one piece?

Yes. You work in rounds from the toe up, leave a chain gap for the heel and add the heel at the end. That way there are no side seams.

02

How does an afterthought heel work?

In one round you loosely chain across half the stitches and skip them. Once the leg and cuff are done, you work rounds with decreases into the opening, much like the toe.

03

How big is the crochet fireplace stocking?

About 20 in (50 cm) tall, about 9 in (23 cm) wide flat, with a foot of about 13.5 in (34 cm). These are approximate sizes for a gauge of 14 hdc × 10 rounds = 4 × 4 in (10 × 10 cm).

04

How much yarn do I need for a big Christmas stocking?

An estimated 450 g (16 oz) of worsted/aran weight yarn in the main color and about 80 g (3 oz) in cream for the cuff. Buy an extra ball to be safe.

05

Which method is easiest?

If you can already crochet in the round, the afterthought heel is easiest because it works like the toe. If you'd rather not work in the round, you can also crochet two flat panels and join them – but then there's a seam.
