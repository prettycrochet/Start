Crochet Wrist Warmers: Free Beginner Pattern for Fingerless Mitts in 3 Sizes

By PrettyCrochet · Published on October 5, 2026 · Reading time: approx. 6 minutes

In short

Wrist warmers (fingerless mitts) are a perfect fall project for beginners: you crochet a rectangle in single crochet through the back loop only, sew the edges together and leave a thumb opening. You need a 3.5–4 mm hook and, as a rough guide, one 50 g ball of medium-weight yarn per pair. Further down you'll find the free pattern with stitch counts for S, M and L.

In October your hands are often cold at the desk or on the bike, but it is still too early for full gloves. That is what wrist warmers are for: they keep your wrists and the backs of your hands warm, leave your fingers free and are done in one cozy evening. Here you'll find yarn and color ideas and a pattern that needs only chains and single crochet.

1. Why are wrist warmers a good fall project?

Wrist warmers are small, wearable and forgiving of little mistakes. They fit the time of year when it is cool but not yet winter. Crochet blogs also recommend boot cuffs and warmers as a quick fall project that is good for layering (see sources).

  • Quick: as a rough guide, a pair takes 2–3 hours.
  • Little yarn: one ball is usually enough – ideal for leftovers and a special single skein.
  • Easy: you only need chain, single crochet and turning – all covered in the crochet basics.
  • Great as a gift: a pair in a favorite color fits into any envelope.

For this pattern use a medium-weight yarn (often labeled DK or light worsted) and a 3.5–4 mm hook. The label gives a recommendation, as I explain in Which yarn for crochet beginners?.

For fall 2026, the knitting and crochet supplier KnitPro names deep rust, ochre, sage, emerald, burgundy and warm neutrals, plus hand-dyed yarns with subtle color shifts (see sources). From that I put together this palette for wrist warmers:

The color swatches are a guide – compare them with real yarn in the shop.

Tip: If you only buy one ball, pick a yarn with a soft color shift. Then the wrist warmers look lively even without a pattern. Make both from the same ball or the same dye lot.

4. What size do you need?

Measure your wrist with a tape measure. The wrist warmers should fit comfortably and stretch a little. The stitch and row counts are guides for about 18 stitches and 20 rows per 4 in (10 cm) (single crochet through the back loop only, 3.5–4 mm hook). Crochet a gauge swatch first and adjust if yours differs.

Pattern: Wrist warmers in single crochet through the back loop

Size
S, M or L (see table)
Time
about 2–3 hours for a pair (rough guide)
Level
Beginner
Yarn
medium-weight yarn (DK/light worsted), approx. 50 g per pair (rough guide; keep 2 balls handy for size L)
Tools
3.5–4 mm hook, scissors, tape measure, tapestry needle

Abbreviations (US terms)

ch = chain · sc = single crochet · BLO = back loop only. All abbreviations are listed in the abbreviations guide. You work in rows and turn at the end of each row. Make two identical wrist warmers.

Wrist warmer (make 2)

  1. Foundation: ch S 23 / M 27 / L 31.
  2. Row 1: sc in the 2nd ch from hook, then sc in each remaining ch (S 22 / M 26 / L 30)
  3. Row 2: ch 1 (does not count as a stitch), turn. Sc BLO in each stitch across (S 22 / M 26 / L 30)
  4. Rows 3 through 30 (S) / 34 (M) / 38 (L): repeat Row 2 (S 22 / M 26 / L 30)

Wrap the piece around your wrist. The two short edges should just meet or stretch slightly. If it is too loose, work a few rows fewer; if it is too tight, a few more. Then cut the yarn, leaving a tail of about 16 in (40 cm).

Sewing up and thumb opening

  1. Fold the rectangle into a tube so that the first row meets the last row.
  2. Sew the two edges together stitch by stitch with the tail (mattress stitch or small whipstitches). Start at the wrist edge.
  3. Leave the thumb opening: size S: sew 5 stitches, leave 6 open, sew the remaining 11. Size M: 6 – 6 – 14. Size L: 7 – 7 – 16.
  4. Weave in all ends. Try it on: the thumb opening should feel comfortable; if not, open one more stitch.
  5. Make the second wrist warmer the same way.

The numbers are guides because yarn and tension vary, so measure as you go.

How can you vary the wrist warmers?

  • Stripes: change color every 4 rows, for example rust and cream. Change colors at the start of a row and weave in the ends along the edge – that edge gets sewn up anyway.
  • Longer: for arm warmers add more chains to the foundation (at about 18 stitches per 4 in / 10 cm, roughly 2 ch for each extra centimeter) and place the thumb opening lower.
  • With texture: later you can try double crochet or bobbles instead of single crochet – for starting out, the plain ribbed look is easiest.

If you want to keep track of your rows, the stitch counter helps: it counts rows and remembers where you stopped.

Frequently asked questions about crochet wrist warmers

01

What is the difference between wrist warmers and arm warmers?

The words are often used loosely: both are crocheted or knitted tubes for the wrist and the back of the hand that leave the fingers free. Wrist warmers are usually shorter, arm warmers reach further up the arm.

02

How much yarn do I need for a pair of wrist warmers?

As a rough guide, one ball of about 50 g of medium-weight yarn is enough for size S and M. For size L or longer warmers, two balls are safer. The exact amount depends on your yarn and tension.

03

Which crochet hook should I use for wrist warmers?

For medium-weight yarn (DK or light worsted) a 3.5–4 mm hook works. Follow the yarn label and crochet a gauge swatch first.

04

How do I make the thumb opening?

You do not sew the edges of the tube all the way up: size S: sew 5 stitches, leave 6 open, sew 11. Size M: 6 – 6 – 14. Size L: 7 – 7 – 16. Start at the wrist edge and try it on as you go.

05

Which colors are trending for fall 2026?

KnitPro names rust, ochre, sage, emerald, burgundy and warm neutrals for fall 2026, as well as hand-dyed yarns with soft color shifts.
