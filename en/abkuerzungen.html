Learn to read

The most important notation

Besides the abbreviations, a few symbols and patterns come up in every pattern. Once you know them, you can read any line.

6 sc

Number before the stitch

The number tells you how many stitches to work in a row: “6 sc” means 6 single crochets, each into the next stitch. If it says “in MR”, you work all 6 into the magic ring.

[ ] x 6

Square brackets with a number: repeat

Work everything inside the brackets as many times as the number says. “[sc, inc] x 6” means: 1 single crochet, then 1 increase – and all of that 6 times.

[ ]

Square brackets after “in the … st”: all in the same stitch

If the brackets have no number but follow an insertion point, you work their entire content into one stitch. “[1 hdc, 3 dc, 1 hdc] in the following st” creates a little petal. I use this notation, for example, in the flowers & leaves pattern.

* … *

Asterisks: repeat a section

The part between the asterisks (or set in italics) is repeated – how often is stated right after it, for example “repeat 4 more times”.

(18 sts)

Round brackets at the end: stitch count

The number in round brackets at the end of a line tells you how many stitches you should have in total after that row or round. Count quickly – that way you'll spot mistakes right away.

Rnd 6–11

Ranges of rows or rounds

“Rnd 6–11” means the instruction applies to rounds 6 through 11, so you work it 6 times in a row.

16 / 21 / 31

Several sizes

If a pattern comes in several sizes, the numbers are separated by slashes – always in the same order, for example small / medium / large. Tip: highlight the numbers for your size before you start.

2nd ch

“in the 2nd ch from the hook”

Count the chains backwards from the hook: the loop on the hook doesn't count, you skip the first chain next to it and insert into the second.

Example

One line step by step

This is what a typical line from my crochet bear pattern looks like:

Rnd 3 [sc, inc] x 6 (18 sts)
Rnd 3

Which round?

This is round 3. At the end of round 2 you had 12 stitches.

sc, inc

What to crochet?

1 single crochet in the next stitch, then 2 single crochets together in the following stitch (increase).

x 6

How often?

Repeat this sequence 6 times – that uses exactly the 12 stitches of the previous round.

(18 sts)

Check

Each repeat creates 3 new stitches, so 6 × 3 = 18 stitches. If your count matches, move on to round 4.

US vs. UK

Don't mix up US and UK terms

Crochet patterns in English use two systems – with the same words for different stitches. The most common mix-up is “double crochet”:

US termUK termGerman
single crochet (sc)double crochet (dc)Feste Masche (fM)
half double crochet (hdc)half treble (htr)Halbes Stäbchen (hStb)
double crochet (dc)treble (tr)Stäbchen (Stb)
treble (tr)double treble (dtr)Doppelstäbchen (dStb)

My English patterns use US terms. For other patterns, look for the abbreviation “sc”: if it appears, the pattern is written in US terms – because the UK system has no “sc”.

For best results

My tips for reading patterns

Read it all once

Read the whole pattern once before you start – that way you know what to expect and no step will surprise you.

Use stitch markers

Mark the start of every round. Especially with spiral rounds, it's easy to lose track otherwise.

Tick off instead of remembering

Print the pattern or note down the rounds and tick off each one you've finished – especially for ranges like “Rnd 6–11”.
